# People illustration brief

## What the reference establishes

Open S05 and C01 from the screenshot index. They show one homepage scene, not a complete commissioned illustration library.

Observed qualities: organic dark outlines; elastic poses and elongated limbs; oversized hands; simple facial detail; black silhouette masses; flat fills; paper/white negative space; people interacting with a laptop, phone or sheet of paper. Color and posture make the scene expressive without photorealistic shading.

The screenshot's cyan/green and coral fills are source observations. The skill's pink/yellow/paper interpretation is a proposed adaptation to the marketplace base. Do not treat stroke width, texture, brushes, exact proportions or a motion system as measured source tokens.

## Art direction for original assets

Create an original person or scene in the same broad visual language, preserving the user's product identity. Do not copy the reference's exact poses, composition, distinctive details or logo. The bundled images remain evidence, not production artwork.

- Organic black contour with human variation and confident endings.
- Expressive silhouettes and meaningful gestures. Keep subjects engaged in a specific action relevant to the screen.
- Restrained flat fills: pink, warm yellow, ink and paper as the starting palette. Use white/paper as an active part of the composition.
- Dark filled shapes can balance light line work; avoid covering the scene with tiny hatch patterns that disappear at UI size.
- No plastic 3D rendering, glossy gradients, photorealistic office workers or interchangeable floating abstract blobs as the main illustration.
- Leave a clean zone for the actual heading and controls. Do not solve overlap by dimming the artwork or adding translucent panels behind every line.
- Keep text, button labels and interface icons out of the artwork. Render those in the real UI so they can wrap, translate and remain accessible.

## Compose for the task

| Use | Composition |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | One wider scene with two or three people; composition supports the message and search, not a giant decorative barrier |
| Compact entry | One person or a tightly composed gesture; simplify or recompose for narrow screens |
| Category orientation | A meaningful action or tool relationship, paired with a real text label |
| Empty results | A calm searching/reframing action; product text explains recovery |
| Confirmation | A small gesture of completion; product text states what actually happened |
| Repeated results / form fields | Usually no person illustration; identity and information already occupy the card |

A minimal asset set can be one entry scene with a compact variant and two small state scenes. Category vignettes are optional. Do not generate a large library before the requested screens need it.

## Reuse, generate, or commission

Inspect existing authorized assets first. Reuse them if they fit the required composition and palette. If new artwork is needed, use the available image-generation workflow or commission original illustration; follow the current tool's skill/documentation. This skill does not require a specific provider or raster/vector pipeline.

For a generated image, a useful adaptable brief is:

> Original editorial people illustration for [screen and product]. Show [specific action] using [meaningful tools]. Organic black ink contours, elastic human silhouettes, simplified facial features, flat pink and warm-yellow fills with generous paper-white space. Place the subject [composition side] and reserve [clear zone] for UI text. No lettering, logos, interface controls, glossy shading or 3D effects. Deliver [required aspect ratio and background treatment].

Choose transparent background when a cutout is actually needed; otherwise compose against the intended paper surface. Use the required output format honestly: do not call a raster image vector or promise editable SVG from a bitmap. Keep the working source plus appropriately sized exports in the receiving project's asset structure.

Inspect the saved output at its actual display size on desktop and narrow layouts. Check hand/face quality, silhouette, crop, text-zone clearance, palette and consistency with other assets. A character that looks good as a large artboard can fail as a small UI vignette.
